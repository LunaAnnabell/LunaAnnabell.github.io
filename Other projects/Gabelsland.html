Art and Technology - 2nd Semester Project

Gabelsland

Rum, Kunst og Teknologi (Space, Art, and Technology)

Role: Art and Technology 2nd Semester Student

Context: Art and Technology Bachelor Program at Aalborg University / Supervisor: Line Marie Bruun Jespersen

Duration: 14/02/2022 – 14/06/2022 (spring-2022)

Elevator Pitch: This project addresses how Gabels Torv in Aalborg functions primarily as an overlooked transit zone or "non-place". To alter the square's pedestrian flow and encourage people to stop and explore, the group designed and installed Gabelsland—a responsive, interactive tunnel installation utilizing natural daylight, mirrors, motorized rainbow-colored cellophane panels, and bird repellent tape.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: Gabels Torv lacks emotional attachment and is treated strictly as a pathway from point A to point B, causing people to ignore its architectural and spatial potential.

Target Audience & Stakeholders: Pedestrians, citizens of Aalborg, and visitors crossing Gabels Torv.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: A customized Triple Diamond design model combining Problem Discovery/Definition, Solution Discovery/Concept Validation, and Design/Development/Delivery.

Analysis & Research: Site analyses using Kevin Lynch's Image of the City, Gehl Architects' 12 quality criteria, Henri Lefebvre’s Spatial Triad, interviews, and ground observations.

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: Processing (for motion-detection via webcam) and Arduino (for motor communication via serial port).

Tools & Architecture: A wooden framework, 5 Dynamixel motors, an overhead webcam tracking pixel color differences, 4 tilted mirrors, laminated rainbow-colored cellophane plates, and reflective bird repellent tape.

5. Solution & Artifacts

Interactive tunnel walkthrough structure where pedestrians passing through trigger webcam-driven motor rotations of colored cellophane panels.

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: Out of roughly 1,300 people observed crossing the square, 65 individuals altered their path or reacted to the installation.

Personal Takeaways: Highlighted that exterior weather conditions heavily impact lightweight materials like cellophane, emphasizing the need for more robust weather-proofing and heavier acrylic materials.
