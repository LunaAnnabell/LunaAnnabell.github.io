Art and Technology - 1st Semester Project (P1)

The Act of Helping

Skulptur & Teknologi (Sculpture & Technology)

Role: Art and Technology 1st Semester Student

Context: Art and Technology Bachelor Program at Aalborg University / Supervisor: Line Marie Bruun Jespersen

Duration: 11/10/2021 – 17/12/2021 (fall-2021)

Elevator Pitch: "The Act of Helping" is an interactive light sculpture designed to raise awareness about mental health struggles and the societal stigma surrounding them. The sculpture remains in an incomplete, cold state with artificial tears until visitors physically insert missing puzzle pieces into its body, which triggers vibrant, pulsing lighting effects that symbolize human potential unlocked through mutual aid and support.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: Mental illness and societal stigmatization create severe barriers between individuals suffering from psychological disorders and the help they deserve, often isolating them and worsening their condition.

Target Audience & Stakeholders: Exhibition attendees and the general public invited to engage with themes of empathy and mental health.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: Iterative design utilizing the Design Thinking (Double Diamond) model, brainstorming, mind mapping, and prototyping.

Analysis & Research: Literature and state-of-the-art reviews (including Steuart Padwick's public art installations), paired with an exhibition evaluation via observations and a 30-respondent questionnaire.

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: Arduino (C++) using FastLED and Adafruit NeoPixel libraries.

Tools & Architecture: Arduino Uno microcontrollers, addressable NeoPixel LED strips, a NeoPixel ring, photoresistors (LDRs), magnetic/metal contact switches, and an ultrasonic sensor.

5. Solution & Artifacts

Humanoid sculpture built over a chicken-wire skeleton covered in papier-mâché from psychology book pages; interactive puzzle mechanism where users insert missing pieces to complete circuits.

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: Questionnaire data and observations showed that cultural stigma initially acted as a barrier, causing visitors to hesitate to touch or interact without peer examples. Once initiated, interaction provoked strong positive emotional responses.

Personal Takeaways: Highlighted the need for more intuitive interaction affordances, as abstract placement caused initial confusion, and demonstrated the importance of lighting conditions for light-dependent sensors.
