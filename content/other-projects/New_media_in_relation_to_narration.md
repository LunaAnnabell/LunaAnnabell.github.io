# New media in relation to narration

An analysis of how new media shapes the narration, stage design, and storyline structure of a theatrical adaptation.

* **Role:** 5th Semester Project Team Member
* **Context:** Department of Communication and Psychology, Art and Technology, Aalborg University
* **Duration:** Handed in December 15, 2023 (fall 2023)

![Luscinia project](../img/luscinia.jpg)

## Overview & Purpose

This project investigates how new media influences the narration, stage design, and storyline structure of a theatrical adaptation. Using *Luscinia: En nats gale fortællinger*, based on H.C. Andersen's *The Nightingale*, as a case study, the report explores postdramatic theatre, media taxonomy, and narrative imagination through physical acting, projection mapping, puppetry, and sound.

## Main Skills & Technology

* **Tech Stack / Tools:** MadMapper, ceiling projectors, stage lamps, follow spots, and synthesizer.
* **Methods / Processes:** Devised theatre methodology, collective contributions, improvisation, an evolving manuscript, and modular documentation.
* **Theoretical Framework:** Hans-Thies Lehmann, Aristotle, Martha Nussbaum, and David Saltz.

## Solution & Result

The project combined a dual storyline structure, custom puppetry and props, and mixed-reality interplay between live actors and prerecorded video installations. The flexible book scenography and new media broke down traditional dramatic linearity and guided the audience through multiple timelines.

The process also showed the importance of explicit role distribution and documentation when working with ambitious multimedia ideas under tight deadlines.
