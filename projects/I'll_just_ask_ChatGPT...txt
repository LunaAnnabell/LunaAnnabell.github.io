Describtion of project:

The study explores the informal role general-purpose chatbots play in health-related information-seeking and well-being management. It addresses the empirical gap surrounding how users formulate concerns, prompt models, and make sense of responses when dealing with unstructured health queries.

Solution and conclusion:

The research methodology comprised 20 semi-structured qualitative interviews featuring a scenario-based chatbot task (evaluating physical symptoms vs. mental well-being scenarios) analyzed using Reflexive Thematic Analysis. The findings generated four core themes: Available and Low-Friction Use, Conversational AI as a Sense-Making Tool, User Agency, and Social Contracts and Privacy. The thesis concludes that chatbots primarily function as low-effort sense-making tools that help users structure vague uncertainties, while simultaneously introducing complex tensions regarding trust, verification, agreeableness, and responsibility
