Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) / App Development

Social Engagement Through Interactive Design

An iterative approach to event discovery

Role: Interaction Designers & Android Developers

Context: 8th-semester student project at Aalborg University (AAU)

Duration: February 2025 – May 2025 (spring, 2025)

Elevator Pitch: Newcomers and university students frequently struggle to build meaningful social connections because established cultural activities rely heavily on informal networks or alcohol-centered events that lack inclusivity. To address this, we developed a user-centered Android application designed to guide users toward local events and venues aligned with their personal interests and lifestyles. By utilizing character-guided paths, interactive timelines, and categorized activity filters, the application bridges the gap between newcomers and local communities to foster organic social engagement.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: Moving to a new city presents severe social hurdles for university students, often compounding stress and increasing the risk of loneliness (which rose to 17% among young adults in Denmark by 2023). Traditional event-discovery platforms (like Facebook or fragmented university websites) suffer from information overload, poor filtering capabilities, and a lack of discoverability for alternative, non-alcohol-centric social opportunities.

Target Audience & Stakeholders: Built primarily for students and newcomers relocating to cities like Aalborg who lack pre-existing social networks, impacting local event organizers, student clubs, and campus communities seeking to engage new members.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: We followed an iterative Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) design framework structured around the Design Thinking Process model (Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test) combined with a Participatory Design approach where users collaborated as active co-creators.

Analysis & Research: Conducted desk research, open group interviews, future workshops, and dot-voting exercises using the 5W1H framework (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How) to transition qualitative insights into structured MoSCoW system requirements.

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: Java, XML, and Android SDK, built within Android Studio utilizing Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) principles and a Layered Architecture Pattern (Model, Function, User Interface).

Tools & Architecture: Figma for high-fidelity prototyping and visual UI design (drawing inspiration from Duolingo and Candy Crush layout paradigms); Clip Studio Paint for custom digital asset creation.

5. Solution & Artifacts

Character Selection & Vibe Profiles, Interactive Timelines & Maps, Integrated Calendar & Details with modular component implementations.

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: Usability testing with target students highlighted successes in visual engagement and layout intuitiveness, while revealing critical pain points regarding gendered character biases and navigation consistency—prompting a second iteration that deprioritized strict character personas in favor of open category exploration.

Personal Takeaways: Deepened our technical proficiency in Java-based Android development, object-oriented composition, and the practical complexities of balancing participatory design feedback with structured UI/UX heuristic evaluations.
