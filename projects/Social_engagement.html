Interaction Design / Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)

Social Engagement Through Interactive Design

An iterative approach to event discovery

Role: UX Designer & Developer

Context: 8th Semester Project

Duration: February 2025 – May 28, 2025 (Spring 2025)

Elevator Pitch: Moving to a new city can be socially challenging for students who lack a pre-existing network. This project explores how an interactive mobile application prototype—connecting users' interests to local events and venues—can guide newcomers toward shared experiences and help them form human relationships.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: New students face academic stress, loneliness, and social barriers, while traditional integration events heavily rely on alcohol, leaving non-drinkers and newcomers isolated. Existing platforms (like AAU pages or Facebook) can be cluttered, hard to navigate, or fail to display relevant events matching personal interests.

Target Audience & Stakeholders: University students and newcomers to Aalborg looking to build social networks and discover local events. This project aims to create a more inclusive and accessible platform for social engagement.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: Design Thinking Process model (Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test) combined with Participatory Design (Future Workshops, open group interviews) and an iterative HCI approach.

Analysis & Research: Desk research, thematic coding of qualitative interview and workshop data, MOSCOW requirements prioritization, and heuristic evaluations based on Nielsen's principles.

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: Android Studio, Java, XML, and Object-Oriented Programming (OOP).

Tools & Architecture: Figma (for prototyping/wireframes), Clip Studio Paint (for custom character art), and an open-strict Layered Architecture Pattern (Model, Function, and User Interface layers).

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: Usability testing with target students highlighted the effectiveness of visual affordances, natural mapping, and character-driven categories, while pointing out areas for improvement such as reducing gender stereotypes in character design and streamlining navigation

Personal Takeaways: Insights into balancing participatory design limitations, applying object-oriented principles via composition ("Has-a" relationships), and managing T-shaped prototype implementation in Android Studio.
