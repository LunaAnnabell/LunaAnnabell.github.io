Art and Technology - 3rd Semester Project (P3)

Symbiotic Wellbeing

Dynamisk kunst & teknologi (Dynamic Art & Technology)

Role: Group 1 Author (Donna Isabella Simonsen, Luna Annabell Linné Jensen, Marie Fritjof Knudsen)

Context: Art and Technology Bachelor Program at Aalborg University / Supervisor: Markus Löchtefeld

Duration: 09/09/2022 – 19/12/2022 (fall-2022)

Elevator Pitch: This project explores mental health and nature's restorative effects by developing "Symbiotic Wellbeing," a sprouting wearable garment made of green yarn embedded with live cress and chia seeds. Integrated with a hidden automated watering system controlled by a Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) sensor and posture/wear detection, the garment explores human-nature relations and mutualism by letting wearers choose whether the living sprouts survive or wither.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: Modern urban lifestyles disconnect humans from nature, adversely affecting mental health (such as stress and anxiety), despite humanity's power over natural ecosystems.

Target Audience & Stakeholders: Exhibition visitors and individuals experiencing mental health pressures or urban disconnection from nature.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: Dual-model framework combining the Double Diamond model (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver) and Damien Newman's The Design Squiggle model for chaotic-to-focused creative progression.

Analysis & Research: Literature studies on Indoor Nature Exposure (INE), mindfulness, somaesthetics, and Donald Norman’s three levels of emotional design, evaluated using an exhibition questionnaire survey and a 2-hour focus group interview.

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: Arduino IDE (C++), AFMotor library, and custom sensor reading logic.

Tools & Architecture: Arduino Uno, an Arduino Motor Shield (Rev3), an air pump connected via tubing to a water reservoir bottle, a Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) sensor, a 6V power supply, and a manually adjustable potentiometer.

5. Solution & Artifacts

Wearable dress featuring crocheted/knitted top structure and supporting corset lined with live ivy; automated watering circuit pumps water whenever GSR sensor detects wear.

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: Questionnaire and focus group results revealed that while the garment was an aesthetic success and brought elements of nature indoors, it did not significantly reduce wearer stress.

Personal Takeaways: Highlighted practical physical challenges in wearable design, such as water tube sizing, uneven sprouting due to temperature/moisture regulation, and the added weight of wet textiles.
