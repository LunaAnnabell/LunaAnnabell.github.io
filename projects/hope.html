Art and Technology - 4th Semester Project

HOPE

Using Autoethnographic Methods to Design an Interactive video Installation

Role: Art and Technology 4th Semester Student

Context: Art and Technology Bachelor Program at Aalborg University

Duration: 13/02/2023 – 31/05/2023 (spring-2023)

Elevator Pitch: This project investigates whether instructing individuals with first-hand experience of the Danish psychiatric system to write autoethnographic self-reflections can serve as a valuable method for artists to design an interactive video installation. The resulting installation, HOPE, uses video, sound, and a physical watering can interaction to convey a patient's emotional journey and feelings of hopelessness or lack of care.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: While autoethnographic research relies on the researchers' own first-hand experiences, researchers may not always share personal experiences with the topic they wish to explore (e.g., systemic issues and long wait times in the Danish psychiatry system).

Target Audience & Stakeholders: Exhibition visitors at Aalborg University, general publics, and individuals affected by the psychiatric system.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: Preliminary semi-structured interviews with 5 people connected to the psychiatric system, analyzed via Grounded Theory coding to identify key themes ("time", "frustration", "help").

Analysis & Research: Application of second-hand autoethnographic self-reflections (combining text and visual expressions) provided by a participant, followed by qualitative/quantitative visitor questionnaires and a dedicated participant evaluation.

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: Arduino IDE, Processing, TouchDesigner, Python, and Open Sound Control (OSC) communication.

Tools & Architecture: An Adafruit BNO055 absolute orientation sensor attached to an M5Stack Atom Motion Kit microcontroller inside a watering can, a projector playing video outputs via Spout/TouchDesigner, a spotlight, and stereo speakers.

5. Solution & Artifacts

Real yellow dahlia flower paired with a red watering can; tilting the watering can triggers a particle-animation watering effect and plays the main video installation.

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: Participant 3 confirmed that the installation successfully captured the clinical indifference, draining atmosphere, and emotional nuance of their experience.

Personal Takeaways: Demonstrated that using second-hand autoethnographic self-reflections is a viable and successful method for artist-researchers to build emotionally resonant work without direct personal experience.
