5th Semester Project / Narratives and Interaction

New media in relation to narration

An analysis of Luscinia, Luscinia: A Night's Mad Stories

Role: 5th Semester Project Team Member

Context: Department of Communication and Psychology, Art and Technology, Aalborg University

Duration: Handed in December 15, 2023 (fall 2023)

Elevator Pitch: This project investigates how new media influences the narration, stage design, and storyline structure of a theatrical adaptation. Using the performance Luscinia, Luscinia: En nats gale fortællinger (based on H.C. Andersen's The Nightingale) as a case study, the report explores postdramatic theatre elements, media taxonomy (David Saltz), and narrative imagination (Martha Nussbaum) to blend physical acting, projection mapping, puppetry, and sound.

2. Problem & Objective

The Challenge: Exploring how integrating new media into theatrical productions can reshape stage design, transition between nonlinear storylines, and foster narrative imagination.

Target Audience & Stakeholders: Viewers of experimental theatre and the external collaboration partner Trekanten – Bibliotek og Kulturhus.

3. Methods & Approach

Development Process: Devised theatre methodology involving collective contributions, improvisations, an evolving manuscript, and a modular "memory-bank" documentation process.

Analysis & Research: Grounded in theatrical theories including Hans-Thies Lehmann's Postdramatic Theatre, Aristotle's Poetics, Martha Nussbaum's Narrative Imagination, and David Saltz's media taxonomy (Sharing the Stage with Media).

4. Technical & Visual Foundation

Tech Stack: MadMapper software controlling ceiling stage lamps and ceiling projectors.

Tools & Architecture: A two-projector setup mapped onto both sides of a movable, over-dimensional wooden book prop, two manually controlled follow spots, 14 ceiling stage lamps, and a synthesizer for sound cues.

5. Solution & Artifacts

Dual Storyline Structure, custom puppetry and props, and Mixed Reality Interplay combining live actors with prerecorded video installations.

6. Results & Key Learnings

Impact & Feedback: The use of new media and a flexible, mobile book scenography successfully broke down traditional dramatic linearity, guiding the audience through multiple timelines and fostering self-reflection.

Personal Takeaways: Highlighted the importance of explicit role distribution and process documentation when navigating tight deadlines and ambitious multi-media visions in devised theatre.
